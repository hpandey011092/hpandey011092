## Writing a POC narrative or closure report for a client

Use this for any POC wrap-up, closure report, scale-up proposal or results deck (first used for Axio, Nov 2026).

### Story
- Titles are action titles. Read in order, they make an elevator pitch: the problem, our solution, the impact, then how we get there. Each slide makes one point, and the body supports that point with data.
- Show the trajectory, not a snapshot. Compare month to month what changed (agent craft, edge cases, workflows, analytics) and what it moved. Working with Blue Machines is ongoing work, not a one-off.
- Close with what we will do next ("scale in December"). Don't offer another test or a smaller POC.

### Tone
- Show positives without putting down any channel: IVR, telecallers, field or the client's own team. Anything that reads as a dig can be taken the wrong way.
- Don't quote the client's internal benchmarks or targets back to them. Their KPIs are framed as "what we propose to track", never "what we know you measure".
- Don't compare to a human benchmark unless the cohort, ticket mix and date window match exactly. If they don't, just state our number.
- Don't comment on whether a cost number is good. State it with its method and let the client judge.

### Metrics
- Lead with the metrics the client cares about: **connect → collect** and **collection efficiency**. For Axio, show APL first.
- Don't headline RPC-based metrics (RPC → collect, RPC → PTP).
- Every number states its date window and denominator.

### Where AI adds value (positives we can measure)
- **Coverage:** calls on Sundays, holidays and days the human team is off. Quote calls, hours and collections on those days.
- **Controllability:** calling can be stopped, paused or terminated within minutes when the client asks. Calling windows can be set by hour. More numbers and channels, or our own telephony, raise concurrency.
- **Transparency and compliance:** every call is recorded, transcribed and scored. The agent stays on script, keeps to attempt caps and RBI calling hours, and never uses a personal phone.
- **Fewer escalations:** one customer state across channels, an instant stop on payment, and context recalled from earlier calls by the follow-up agent.

### Sources and labels
- Each slide carries a source line using only: "Blue Machines internal analysis", "Minutes of meeting with <client>, <date>", "Blue Machines proposal", "market analysis", or a regulator circular.
- Never name Slack, Granola or vendor reports in client material.
- Tag slides meant only for the internal team as INTERNAL. Leave client-facing slides untagged. Put no internal caveats in speaker notes: anyone the file is shared with can read them.

### Format
- Follow the client's own template: fonts, colours, light or dark background. Use text monikers and simple icons rather than pasted logos. Keep it sparse, and move commentary to appendix slides.
- Deliver as Google Slides so people can comment, and keep earlier versions instead of overwriting them.
